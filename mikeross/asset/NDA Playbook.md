Clause by Clause Breakdown

Preamble
Negotiation Points

exclamation markIssue: Other party queries the use of Bolt Operations OU as the signatory. 

megaphoneTalking Point: Bolt requires that all third parties contract with Bolt Operations OÜ in respect of our standard NDA. Bolt Operations OÜ is the main operating entity of the Bolt group and we want to ensure that the main operating legal entity enters into NDAs. The definition of "Affiliates" (in Section 1) in the NDA includes all subsidiaries (such as those in the other international locations) as well as Estonian parent company Bolt Technology OÜ, making sure that (i) confidential information disclosed by us includes information relating to such other group companies and (ii) any information received by us can be shared with such other group companies. The definition of Affiliates is also mutual on the third party so will encompass their subsidiaries.

fire Fallback Option: Preferably we maintain Bolt Operations. However, if this becomes a deal-breaker and you cannot negotiate otherwise, then at your commercial discretion you may use another Bolt group company. However, note that this then results in a more narrow scope of protection which may only adequately cover sharing from the local subsidiary and on a local level. 


Purpose
exclamation markIssue: The other party wants a purpose included in the NDA. 

megaphoneTalking Point: Bolt considers that all information that it discloses will be Confidential Information and therefore it does not require the parties to specifically designate a purpose for the disclosure as long as the other party undertakes to maintain the confidentiality undertaking.

fire Fallback Option: There may be unique circumstances where a “Purpose” may be required by the other party. In such a case, at the end of Section 1 the following amendment option is permissible and can be added to the agreement by counsel: “The Confidential Information disclosed for the purposes of this Agreement relates specifically to [INSERT SPECIFIC PROJECT PURPOSE].” Please make a service desk ticket to legal advising the need for this change and please fill in the above sentence with a clear purpose statement. 


Definition of Affiliates
exclamation markIssue 1: The other party wishes to share the Confidential Information with other parties such as employees, agents, professional consultants, accountants and legal advisors or individuals “that need to know” and what to define this in the NDA. 

megaphoneTalking Point: Bolt does not require the other party to list all of the associated parties that it wishes to disclose the Bolt Confidential Information to as our NDA wording is sufficiently broad to permit such discloses to a third party of the other party: “Confidential Information” means all non-public information relating to Bolt or disclosed by Bolt or its Affiliates to the above reference company, its Affiliates or any third party of the foregoing, that is designated as confidential or that, given the nature of the information of the circumstances surrounding its disclosure, reasonably should be considered confidential. However, please be reminded that you are obligated to maintain the confidentiality undertakings when disclosing to a third party.

fire Fallback Option: None. Direct the other party to Section 4 (Company Personnel) and the last sentence which permits an extension to additional third parties who may be the other party of the Confidential Information.

exclamation markIssue 2: Other party wishes to limit one or both parties Affiliates. 

megaphoneTalking Point: Bolt’s corporate structure is organized in a manner that is both flat and complex, consisting of many companies/entities which are fluid enough that Bolt needs entities to share information freely in order to conduct business.  If we are limited to a single entity in a particular NDA, we are forced to request permission to share the Confidential Information or explicitly include additional entities in the existing NDA if we want to enhance a service or expand the business to involve another entity, and there is an additional risk that information would be shared with an Affiliate by someone not familiar with the NDA restriction. Additionally, we would prefer to have a broad enough NDA in place that we can freely use it across different business teams – especially when we’ve devoted time to negotiating agreeable terms.

fire Fallback Option: None

exclamation markIssue 3: Other party requests to identify either or both parties’ Affiliates. 

megaphoneTalking Point: The “Affiliate” definition is standard and with hundreds of subsidiaries we can’t accept the administrative burden of listing all Affiliates. 

fire Fallback Option: None

exclamation markIssue 4: Other party requests to add threshold (50%) for Affiliates.  

megaphoneTalking Point: Bolt’s definition of Affiliate is based on the industry standard for describing corporate affiliates.  We can’t know what a company’s corporate structure is, and therefore can’t know the risk we take on when the company tries to narrow the definition. There are also corporate structures where there is control, but no ownership. When we are disclosing Confidential Information, we need to know that our information is protected. 

fire Fallback Option: You may elect to amend the clause to state that: “The term “control” means the possession, direct or indirect, of the power to direct or cause the direction of the management and policies of an entity, whether through the ownership of voting securities or otherwise.” Please make a service desk ticket to legal advising the need for this change if you have commercial approval to make it.

exclamation markIssue 5: Other party is an entity that does not have authority to bind its Affiliates.  

fire Fallback Option: None (unless a governmental entity in which cases may be considered through legal SD).

exclamation markIssue 1: Other party wishes to expand or contract the definition of Confidential Information.

exclamation markIssue 2: Other party wishes to impose a marking requirement for Confidential Information.

fire Fallback Option: Limited amendments may be available subject to legal approval. Sometimes a party will require that Confidential Information be marked “Confidential” in order to qualify as Confidential Information. Generally, Bolt does not accept this request because of the administrative burden it puts on the business owner, particularly where we are disclosing Confidential Information. Our internal policies/guidelines have not established this practice for employees to follow consistently. If necessary (strong business need and ownership to comply), we can accept this request but we may want to suggest that we carve out a defined or limited purpose of the NDA before accepting a marking requirement (e.g. specific meeting, business proposal, etc. to limit the marking requirement).  If marking and notification is agreed to Section 8 must be left intact (return and destroy of Confidential Information).  If Bolt will not be disclosing information then this requirement is much less problematic, but the NDA will need to be limited in scope. 

If a deal-breaker, please suggest the following to the counterparty as an alternative: “Information disclosed orally or visually to the other party and identified at the time as proprietary or confidential shall be covered hereunder if it is reduced to writing, marked as confidential, and transmitted to the Receiving Party within thirty (30) days after disclosure.” 

If agreed, please make a service desk ticket to legal advising the need for this change if you have commercial approval to make it.

NDA isn’t intended to cover information that can’t be protected as a trade secret.

Return of Confidential Information
 exclamation markIssue 1: Other party states that they may be under a professional or regulatory obligation to retain the Confidential Information.

megaphoneTalking Point: Bolt will accept the non-return of Confidential Information if you have a regulatory obligation to maintain our Confidential Information. However, the confidentiality undertaking shall continue to survive.

fire Fallback Option: Other party may suggest they have a “professional obligation” as an alternative to the regulatory circumstance but we should test if there is no evidence to suggest this is the case. The other party will need to clearly explain the use case and the Bolt legal team member should verify that the other party use case is valid in respect of the applicable regulation. If it can be confirmed then the following can be added to the end of the section: “…unless otherwise agreed by Bolt, Company may (i) retain the Confidential Information as required by law or a regulatory obligation [including for audits] and (ii) copies of the Confidential Information held on IT back-up systems of the Receiving Party. ”

In Section 11 include Section “8” as part of the Agreement that shall survive following termination:

“…with respect to Confidential Information will survive for 3 years following termination of this Agreement, and Sections 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, and 12 will survive indefinitely and the confidentiality obligations of this Agreement…”

Please make a service desk ticket to legal advising the need for this change if you have commercial approval to make it.

exclamation markIssue 2: Other party seeks to retain Confidential Information.

fire Fallback Option: In general, Bolt maintains an extremely strong position against another party retaining a copy of any of our Confidential Information after the business purpose is completed or terminated.  We should ask what the purpose would be for them to keep our Confidential Information. Our concern is that if a party keeps a copy of our Confidential Information, in any form, and our relationship ends, the copy of the information they’ve kept may be subject to subpoena or lawsuit for which we no longer have business purpose/relationship in connection with.  We do require the destruction or return of emails.  But we don’t require a scrub of backup media/hard drives/servers. We should not allow companies to keep a copy of our Confidential Information for “compliance with the NDA” or for “evidentiary purposes”.  

If the request is well evidenced and validated, please make a service desk ticket to legal advising the need for this change if you have commercial approval to make it.


Injunctive Relief
exclamation markIssue: The other party wants a limitation on liability in this NDA to manage their risks. Bolt does not offer a limitation on liability in this NDA as this is a non-transactional document where there is no flow of money. 

megaphoneTalking Point: Our primary concern is to seek an immediate injunction to prevent further non-disclosures and your co-operation in every reasonable way to help Bolt regain possession of such non-disclosure of the Confidential Information and prevent its further unauthorized use as specified in Section 5. With this in mind, trying to impose a limitation on liability is not necessarily the correct remedy for a breach of a non-disclosure agreement as this is not the immediate remedy that Bolt would take in the event of a breach. We do not want the parties to rely solely on financial compensation as the remedy for the breach. This is why we cannot accept your proposal for a limitation on liability.  Furthermore, there is no transaction (flow of money) between the parties therefore it is not equitable for the parties to rely on a limitation on liability. 

fire Fallback Option: If the position of not having a cap on liability is a showstopper, then escalate to legal through service desk for consideration.
